## Key Reasons for Multiple Pricing Tiers
Here's why having tiers, ranging from a "full baseline" to "custom pricing with extensive features," is a strategic choice by Adino:

### 1. Market Segmentation and Accessibility
Pricing tiers allow Adino to offer a product at a price point suitable for everyone from small organizations (who might only need the baseline package) to large enterprises (who require the extensive, custom features). 

The lowest tier, with its simple pricing, making it easier for new or budget-conscious customers to try and adopt the software.

### 2. Value-Based Pricing and Revenue Maximization
Customers who use the software for mission-critical operations or who need highly specialized features derive more value from the product. Higher tiers ensure Adino captures that increased value, rather than charging a flat rate that undervalues the extensive features.

A tiered structure provides a clearer model for forecasting revenue, as customers can track customer movement between tiers and understand the financial impact of feature releases or changes.

### 3. Clear Upgrade Path (The "Land and Expand" Strategy)
A customer can start with the simple, baseline tier. As their business grows, their needs become more complex, or they require more users, the pre-defined, mid-level tiers offer an obvious and easy-to-understand path to access more powerful features or capacity.

Offering an upgrade path means customers are less likely to leave the platform when their needs evolve; they simply move to a higher-priced Adino tier.
